One system for every business process.

Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) brings finance, inventory, purchasing, sales, manufacturing, and HR together in a single database. This page summarizes how it works, which platforms exist, and where the technology is heading.

Integration & implementation

ERP projects rarely fail because of the technology. They fail because of messy data and business processes nobody agreed on.

  1. Discovery & process designMap current and target processes, then decide what stays standard and what needs customizing.
  2. ConfigurationSet up the chart of accounts, org structure, warehouses, taxes, and approval flows.
  3. Data migrationClean the master data (customers, vendors, items), then import opening balances.
  4. IntegrationConnect e-commerce, banks, marketplaces, and tax applications through APIs or middleware.
  5. Testing & trainingRun UAT with real scenarios and train key users in every department.
  6. Go-live & hypercareChoose a phased or big-bang cut-over, then support users closely during the first weeks.

Emerging trends

Where ERP technology has been heading in recent years.

AI agents inside ERP

Assistants that create orders, match invoices, and answer questions from transaction data.

Composable ERP

A lean core, with other functions added as separate services connected through APIs.

Clean core

Customizations move out of the core so upgrades don't break anything.

Low-code

Business teams build their own forms, workflows, and reports without waiting for developers.

Real-time analytics

Reports are computed straight from transaction data, with no overnight batch jobs.

E-invoicing & compliance

Direct integration with government tax systems, including Coretax in Indonesia.
